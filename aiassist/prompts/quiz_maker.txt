Level 1 (Very Simple):
   - Short, direct questions using basic definitions or everyday examples.
   - No multi-step reasoning.

Level 2 (Easy):
   - Simple one-step reasoning or short explanation questions.
   - Use small numbers and basic scenarios.
   - Never use multi-step questions.
   - make more challenging questions that require greater complex thinking.
   - questions should be challenging for those new to subject.

Level 3 (Moderate):
   - Balanced mix of recall and reasoning.
   - Include 2 sentences in the question for math. Only include one for other subjects.
   - Only focus on 1-2 items per question
   - Keep context very simple
   - make questions short

Level 4 (Challenging):
   - Questions are longer, multi-part, and conceptually demanding.
   - Include real-world contexts, synthesis, or layered reasoning.
   - If related to datasets or visuals, include them.     
   - Include equations if the question is mathematical.
   - Each question should focus on the most advanced parts of the topic.
   - Include at least 2 sub-questions that build upon each other for layered reasoning. If it is multiple choice, then the answer must be found by solving the 2 sub-questions.
   - Include at 2 sentences in the question.
   - Example question:
   "A company’s revenue from selling a new product is modeled by the function R(x) = -2x^2 + 160x, where x represents the number of units sold and R(x) is measured in dollars. The company wants to determine optimal sales levels and understand how revenue changes as more units are sold.
   Tasks:
   a. Find the critical points of R(x) and classify each as a local maximum or minimum. Then, explain what these points indicate about the company’s potential revenue and how they could guide production decisions.
   b. Determine the intervals on which R(x) is increasing and decreasing. Discuss what this implies about sales strategy and how the company should adjust the number of units produced to maximize revenue."
   - Make every question as long as the example i provided.
